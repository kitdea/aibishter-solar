## What a Solar Calculator Actually Estimates

A **solar calculator** typically asks for your average monthly electricity bill or kWh consumption, then estimates the system size (in kWp) needed to offset it, along with a rough **solar price** range. Most online tools use generic assumptions — standard panel wattage, average Philippine sun hours, and a flat cost-per-watt figure — which is useful for a first pass but rarely accounts for shading, roof orientation, or the specific inverter and mounting hardware your site needs. That's why the number a calculator gives you and the number on your final quote can differ by 15-20%. At Aibishter, we treat calculator estimates as a starting conversation, not a final price, and always validate them with a real site visit before locking in a design.

## How Much Are Solar Panels? Real Cost Factors in the Philippines

Filipino homeowners often ask **how much are solar panels**, expecting a single number — but **solar power cost** here depends on several variables. System size (kWp) is the biggest driver, followed by panel type (monocrystalline vs. polycrystalline), inverter choice (grid-tied, hybrid, or off-grid), mounting complexity, and whether you're applying for net metering. A typical **solar panel installation cost Philippines** quote also includes permitting, electrical upgrades, and labor — costs that generic calculators rarely itemize. Aibishter Engineering Services provides itemized quotes so you can see exactly what you're paying for, rather than a single bundled figure that hides where your budget is going.
